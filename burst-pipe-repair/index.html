Burst and frozen pipe repair

Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Oak Ridge, TN

A burst pipe can move gallons a minute into a wall or ceiling. The first job is to stop the water. The second is to repair the pipe so it stays fixed, and we handle both.

When a pipe bursts in Oak Ridge, TN, minutes matter more than anything else. Our emergency line answers day or night, including holidays, and the dispatcher starts with the question that counts: can you reach the main shutoff?

Stop the water in the first five minutes

  1. Find the main shutoff, usually near the water meter, at the street side of the house or where the line enters the basement or crawlspace.
  2. Turn the valve clockwise until it stops. A gate valve takes several full turns, and a ball valve takes a quarter turn.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain what is left in the pipes.
  4. Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.
  5. Call us, then move belongings out of the wet area and take photos for your insurer.

If you cannot find the shutoff, or the valve will not turn, say so on the call. Many older homes have a stuck valve, and the City of Oak Ridge can close the meter valve for an emergency. The dispatcher can tell you who to call.

Why pipes burst

Freezing is the best-known cause. Water expands when it freezes, and the pressure splits the pipe, usually where the ice plug sits and not where the ice formed. Other causes are corrosion on old galvanized or copper lines, high water pressure and a joint that finally gave out after years of movement.

Winter in the Tennessee valleys

East Tennessee is not Minnesota, which is exactly why freeze damage catches people off guard. Homes in the Oak Ridge area that sit on crawlspaces or have supply lines in outside walls and garages can freeze during a hard cold snap. A line that has frozen once is likely to do it again unless the cause, such as missing insulation or a drafty vent, gets fixed.

If you suspect a frozen line that has not burst, open the faucet slightly, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer and call us if water does not return. Never use an open flame, because it can start a fire or damage the pipe.

What the repair visit includes

  • Locating the break and shutting off the affected branch so you have water elsewhere.
  • Cutting out the damaged section and replacing it with new pipe and fittings.
  • Testing the line under pressure before closing anything up.
  • A look at the pressure and at nearby pipe that may fail next.
  • Notes and photos you can hand your insurance adjuster.

If the break is behind drywall or under a slab, we may need to open the wall. We tell you where we plan to cut, why and what it will cost before we do. A hidden break that is hard to find may call for leak detection first.

Water damage and insurance

We stop the leak and fix the pipe. Drying walls and floors is a separate job, and a restoration company handles it. Call your insurer promptly, because many policies expect you to limit further damage and to document it. Keep the invoice, the photos and any parts we remove if the adjuster asks.

What affects the price

The price follows access, pipe material, how much pipe fails, the hour of the visit and whether a wall or ceiling has to be opened. A visible split in a basement is the simplest. A break inside a slab is the most involved. You will hear the number first, and we will not go beyond it without asking.

Replacement pipe and fittings follow the standards for their material, such as ASTM B88 for copper tube and ASTM F876 for PEX, and the joints are made as the local plumbing code requires. We pressure test the repair before anything is closed up.
