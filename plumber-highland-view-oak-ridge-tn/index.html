Highland View | Oak Ridge, Anderson County

Plumber in Highland View, Oak Ridge, TN

Highland View is a residential neighborhood in north-central Oak Ridge, near Hillside Road and Outer Drive. Parts of it began as wartime housing that was meant to be temporary, so many houses have a long repair history.

Where Highland View is

Local news describes Highland View as a north-central Oak Ridge neighborhood. Streets named in its coverage include Hillside Road, Outer Drive near New York Avenue, Wainwright Road, Waddell Place and Hunter Circle. It is also the name of an Oak Ridge voting precinct.

Highland View is not a place with published boundaries, so we describe it by those streets. If you are not sure whether your address counts, give us your street and the nearest cross street.

Wartime roots

A National Park Service historic district form, compiled in an earlier survey of the town, records multi-family frame houses and apartment buildings in the Highland View area. The old Highland View School building is now the Children's Museum of Oak Ridge.

The same form describes TVA-designed houses on Outer Drive, west of the school, many of which were refurbished after the war and have outlasted their original six-year life expectancy. That survey is old, and houses have changed since.

Why history changes how we work

Houses built as temporary wartime housing and then kept for decades have usually been altered many times. Additions, removed walls and swapped fixtures leave lines in places a drawing would not show. We cannot say what any one house has, so the plumber looks first and explains the options.

If you know of past remodels, additions or heater moves, tell the dispatcher when you call. That history helps the plumber decide where to look first.

Plumbing services Highland View homeowners ask about

  • Damp walls, stains and unexplained bills, through leak detection and burst pipe repair.
  • Slow or backed-up drains, through drain cleaning.
  • Water heater failures and replacements, through water heater repair and replacement.

In houses that have been altered over the years, water heaters often end up in a tight closet, a crawlspace or a corner of the basement. Tell us the fuel, gas or electric, and roughly how much room there is. That lets the plumber plan the right size and bring the right fittings on the first visit.

Questions to ask before you approve a repair

  • What caused the problem, and how did you confirm it?
  • What are the options, from a quick fix to a lasting one?
  • What does the price cover, and what could change it?
  • If a wall or floor has to be opened, what will it cost to close it again?

Those questions apply to any house, and they matter more in one with a long repair history, where the first look rarely shows everything.

What helps the plumber

Tell us what has been remodeled, where the shutoff is and when the problem started. Clear a path to the heater, the sink base or the cleanout. Photograph damage before cleaning up.
