Emory Valley | Oak Ridge, Anderson County

Plumber in Emory Valley, Oak Ridge, TN

Emory Valley is a residential neighborhood in Oak Ridge that the city treats as its own planning area. Here we focus on drains and sewer lines, since the city has studied the public sewer in this area.

How the city describes Emory Valley

The City of Oak Ridge Community Development Department included Emory Valley, along with the Briarcliff and Lakeview areas, in its City Blueprint planning work. In 2017 the city held an open house at Jefferson Middle School, on Fairbanks Road, so families in those residential neighborhoods could suggest improvements.

Emory Valley is also a voting precinct, which it shares with Hendrix Creek and Woodland in County Commission District 8. We use the neighborhood name with Emory Valley Road as the anchor and do not claim exact boundaries.

Public sewer testing in the neighborhood

In June 2012 city crews smoke-tested the sanitary sewer system in Emory Valley, a method that sends harmless smoke through the pipes to show where leaks and openings are. The testing was part of an ongoing study.

That is a public sewer fact, not a statement about any house. But it is a useful reminder that the main line and the line from your house to it are two different things, with different owners.

Sorting out the private line from the public line

When a drain backs up, the plumber first checks whether the problem is inside the house, in the line you own or in the main. A camera inspection answers that quickly. We tell you what we see and what the options are before any work starts.

If the failure is on the public side, contact the City of Oak Ridge utility office at (865) 425-3400 and we will work with what the city requires. We have no information on housing age in Emory Valley, so we make no assumption about pipe material.

What a camera inspection shows

A small camera on a flexible cable goes into the line through a cleanout. The monitor shows the pipe wall, any roots, standing water, a sagging section or a break. The plumber can mark the spot on the surface and tell you how deep it lies.

That picture lets you choose between cleaning, a repair or no work at all. It also gives you something concrete to show the city if the problem is on the public side.
