Plumbing Installation in Oak Ridge, TN

New fixtures, a remodeled bathroom or a moved kitchen sink all need plumbing done right behind the walls. We install it, connect it and test it so the finished room works.

Plumbing is the part of a remodel that must be right before anyone covers it. A supply line in the wrong spot or a drain with too little slope costs far more to fix after the tile goes down. We handle the rough-in and the finish work.

What we install

  • Sinks, faucets, tubs, showers and toilets.
  • Supply and drain lines for new or moved fixtures.
  • Dishwasher, washing machine and ice maker hookups.
  • Outdoor spigots and hose bibs.
  • Water filtration and softener connections, where you choose them.
  • Shutoff valves, pressure regulators and expansion tanks.

For water heaters, see water heater installation. For disposals, see garbage disposal.

Rough-in and finish: two visits, one plan

Plumbing happens in two stages. Rough-in sets the supply and drain lines inside open walls and floors. The finish stage, after walls and floors are done, sets the fixtures and connects them. Between the two is an inspection in many places.

Older homes and remodel surprises

Opening a wall in an older Oak Ridge house can reveal old galvanized lines, patched drains or vents that were never right. Before we price a bathroom move, we look at what is behind the wall where we can, and we tell you when a hidden condition may add cost. If the supply pipes are in poor condition, see repiping.

Cast iron and clay drains in some older homes do not accept a new connection as easily as modern pipe. We use the right couplings and tie in without stressing the old line.

Venting and drainage

Every drain needs a vent so wastewater can flow. Venting rules affect where a sink or toilet can sit, and they limit how far a drain can run. A good plan checks the vent path early, because a fixture that gurgles or drains slowly after a remodel is usually a venting or slope problem.

Permits and code

Most plumbing installations need a permit, and the local office sets the rules. We follow them, schedule inspections and install to the manufacturer's instructions. If you are planning a project, ask the City of Oak Ridge building office about the process, or tell us your address and we will check.

What a good installation visit looks like

  1. We review the plans or the room with you and mark fixture locations.
  2. You get a written price covering the plumbing and what it excludes.
  3. The rough-in is done and tested while the walls are open.
  4. After inspection and finishes, we set the fixtures and test everything.
  5. We walk you through shutoffs and care for the new fixtures.

What affects the price

Fixture count, distance from existing lines, wall and floor access, pipe materials and permit work set the price. A like-for-like swap costs less than a relocation. You get a written number first.

Settle these before the walls close

A few decisions are cheap on paper and costly after tile. Choose the fixtures before rough-in, because each one has its own rough-in dimensions. Decide where the shutoffs will sit and whether you want them reachable without opening a cabinet. Ask for access panels where a valve or a cleanout will live behind a wall.

We walk the room with you, mark each location and note anything that could change the plan, such as a joist in the way of a drain.

Installations follow the local plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and electrical hookups follow the National Electrical Code (NEC). The local office decides which edition applies, and we work to it.
