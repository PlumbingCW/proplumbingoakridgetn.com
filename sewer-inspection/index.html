Sewer Camera Inspection in Oak Ridge, TN

A camera on a flexible cable shows what is happening inside a drain or sewer line. It replaces guessing with a picture, which is why we use it before most major sewer work and before buying a house.

Most sewer problems are invisible until a drain backs up. A sewer camera inspection in Oak Ridge, TN lets you see the pipe from inside, so you learn where it fails and how badly before you pay for repairs.

What the camera can show

  • Roots entering at joints.
  • Cracks, breaks and offset joints.
  • Bellies or sags where water sits.
  • Grease, scale and debris that narrow the line.
  • The pipe material and the approximate depth.
  • Connections, cleanouts and where a lateral ties in.

A locator at the surface follows the camera head, which gives us the depth and the position of a defect. That information lets us dig a small area, not a whole yard.

Three reasons people book an inspection

The first is a recurring clog. If a cable clears it and it returns in weeks, the camera shows why. The second is a home purchase. A line problem can cost many thousands of dollars, and a standard home inspection does not always look inside the sewer. The third is planning, such as before landscaping, a remodel or a major repair.

Older housing and what the camera tends to find

Oak Ridge grew fast in the 1940s, and later decades added more housing. Different eras used different pipe, from clay and cast iron to Orangeburg fiber pipe and later plastic. Each ages differently. A camera look tells you which one you have and what condition it is in, which matters when you are deciding whether to repair or wait.

Not every older line is a problem. We have seen the pipe from old homes in good shape and the pipe of newer homes with defects. That is why we look and report what is on the video, not what the age implies.

What you receive

  1. A walk through the findings while the video plays on our monitor.
  2. A recording of the run and photos of any defects.
  3. A plain-language summary: what is wrong, what is fine and what we would do.
  4. Repair options with prices if a defect needs work.

If a defect is found, the next page to read is sewer line repair. If the line is clear, you have a baseline to compare against later.

Limits worth knowing

A camera cannot get past a full collapse or a blocked section, and it cannot see through a heavy grease coating, so we sometimes clean the line first. A camera also does not test water pressure or find a supply leak. For that, see leak detection.

What affects the price

Length of line, access and whether the line must be cleaned before the run drive the price. An inspection at a cleanout is the simplest. If there is no cleanout, we may need to pull a toilet or find another access point. You hear the price first.

Findings are described in plain terms, but the repair rules come from the local plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). If a defect needs work, we explain which code point it touches.
