Pine Valley | Oak Ridge, Anderson County

Plumber in Pine Valley, Oak Ridge, TN

Pine Valley was Neighborhood One, one of the first three planned neighborhoods in wartime Oak Ridge. Houses here have had more than 80 years of owners and repairs, so we look before we quote.

One of the original three neighborhoods

The National Park Service records that the first phase of Oak Ridge housing created three neighborhoods. Pine Valley was Neighborhood One, Elm Grove was Two and Outer Drive was Three. Each had single-family houses, a small commercial center and an elementary school, and Pine Valley's school opened in December 1943.

The townsite sits on the east slope of Black Oak Ridge. Pine Valley is also the name of a voting precinct, which is one more sign that residents use the name today. We do not claim exact boundaries and use the neighborhood name with its anchor roads.

The houses that started it

The earliest single-family houses were prefabricated cemesto homes, Types A through E, shipped from Indiana. The Park Service reports about 3,000 were built in 1943, roughly one every 30 minutes. Cemesto panels were bonded cement and asbestos fiberboard joined by wooden studs.

Later phases added frame houses. Since the 1950s, owners have remodeled, added rooms and replaced systems, so what stands today is not what the planners drew.

What older construction means when a wall has to be opened

We cannot say what is inside the walls of any one house. Because the Park Service describes the original panels as containing asbestos, we suggest a simple rule for any older house. Do not cut or drill into old wall panels yourself, and ask the plumber how an opening will be handled before work starts.

Many houses have been remodeled since, and an addition may have different materials than the original. That is why the plumber starts with what is visible and tells you if more of the house needs to be opened.

Services Pine Valley homeowners ask about

  • Supply lines and fittings that have been added to or changed over many years, through repiping and leak detection.
  • Water heater swaps in a closet or a crawlspace, covered by water heater replacement and installation.
  • Slow drains and sewer odors, handled through drain cleaning and sewer inspection.

What a good estimate in an older house includes

A fair estimate names the problem, the part of the system it affects and the work needed to fix it. It should also say what is outside the scope. In an older house, ask whether the price assumes the walls open easily and what happens if they do not.

General plumbing practice also calls for permits where the local code office requires them. Ask the plumber whether your job needs one, and confirm with the City of Oak Ridge if you are unsure.

Ask before you decide on a bigger job

A house with eight decades of history can hide a lot. Before agreeing to a repipe or a major line replacement, ask the plumber what they saw, what the options are and what the price covers. We follow that approach on every call.
