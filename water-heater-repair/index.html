Water heater repair

Emergency Water Heater Repair in Oak Ridge, TN

Cold showers, a pilot that will not stay lit or water around the base of the tank all have a cause we can find. Our plumbers diagnose first, repair what can be repaired and tell you plainly when a repair no longer makes sense.

No hot water or a leak: what to do first

Losing hot water on a cold Oak Ridge morning feels like an emergency, and sometimes it is. A leaking tank can flood a utility room, and a gas smell is dangerous. Our 24/7 line takes water heater repair calls at night and on weekends, and the first question we ask is whether you see water or smell gas.

If water is leaking, shut off the cold supply to the heater and the power or gas control. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility first. If there is no leak and no smell, the fault is usually one of a short list.

Leaks: fitting or tank

Most leaks start at the top of the heater, where the supply lines, the relief valve and the anode rod connect. We trace the water to its source before we touch anything. A dripping fitting is a small repair. A weep from the bottom of the shell is the end of the tank, and we will explain that plainly and point you to water heater replacement.

The temperature and pressure relief valve deserves attention too. It is a safety device, and if it drips or discharges, something upstream may be wrong, such as high pressure or an overheated tank. We test it and check the water pressure rather than just swapping the valve.

What older Oak Ridge homes add to the picture

A good share of Oak Ridge housing dates to the 1940s through the 1960s, and many owners have swapped heaters more than once. Over the years, closets get repurposed and flues get shortened. We check the flue, the clearances and the combustion air on gas units, because a repair is the time to catch an unsafe setup.

If your water tastes metallic or you see scale on faucets, test your tap or ask the City of Oak Ridge for its latest water quality report. We have not verified a hardness figure for the city, so we do not quote one. Scale does shorten heater life and is worth ruling out.

Before you call, check these four things

  • The breaker or fuse for an electric heater.
  • The red reset button on the upper thermostat, if your model has one.
  • Whether the gas valve is open and the pilot window shows a flame.
  • Whether other taps in the house also run cold or only one.

Flushing and care that extend life

Sediment builds on the tank floor and insulates the water from the burner or lower element. Flushing the tank on the schedule in the manufacturer's manual keeps that layer thin. The anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and checking it every few years can add useful life.

We can flush the tank during a repair visit and report the anode condition. If the rod is nearly gone and the tank is old, that is useful information for deciding between a new installation now or a repair and a few more years.

How we price the repair

The part, the access and the time of day drive the price. A thermostat or element swap is a modest repair on an accessible electric heater. A gas control valve or a tight attic install costs more. We confirm the price before work starts and tell you if the repair is not worth the money.

The temperature and pressure relief valve on a tank meets ANSI Z21.22, and we replace it only with one rated for your tank. Repairs follow the manufacturer's instructions and the local plumbing code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
