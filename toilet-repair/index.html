Toilet repair and replacement

Emergency Toilet Repair in Oak Ridge, TN

An overflowing toilet is urgent, and a running one wastes water all day. We clear clogs, fix leaks at the base and replace worn parts or the whole toilet when that is the better call.

A toilet is the one fixture a household cannot go without. When it overflows, runs nonstop or leaks onto the floor, you need help that day. Our emergency line in Oak Ridge, TN is open all hours.

Overflowing toilet: what to do first

  1. Lift the tank lid and press the rubber flapper down to stop more water entering the bowl.
  2. Turn the shutoff valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.
  3. Do not flush again, and keep the lid closed to avoid splashing.
  4. Use a flange plunger, not a cup plunger, with firm and steady strokes.
  5. If the water keeps rising or other drains gurgle, call us.

A toilet that clogs alone is a local problem. When the bathtub or floor drain also backs up, the trouble is deeper in the line, and our sewer line cleaning page covers that.

Water at the base is not a small leak

Water around the base means the seal between the toilet and the drain has failed. Over time that water soaks the subfloor and can rot it. If the floor feels soft near the toilet, stop using it and call. Replacing a wax ring is quick. Replacing a rotted subfloor is not.

Older homes and older toilets

Toilets in older Oak Ridge homes may use more water per flush than modern units, and old flanges can be corroded or set at the wrong height. A newer toilet that uses less water per flush can lower your bill, and we can explain which ones suit your drain. If your home has older drain pipe, a toilet that clogs often may reflect the line and not the fixture.

Habits that prevent clogs

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste.
  • Keep wipes, even flushable ones, in the trash.
  • Use a small amount of paper per flush.
  • Teach children that toys and cotton balls do not belong in the bowl.

Repair or replace

A toilet that is cracked, leaks from the tank or bowl or needs frequent repairs should be replaced. A toilet that needs one cheap part usually should not. We look at the age, the condition and the cost of parts, then tell you which makes sense.

What a replacement includes

  • Removing and disposing of the old toilet.
  • Checking and repairing the flange and subfloor access.
  • A new wax ring or seal and new supply line.
  • Setting, leveling and testing the toilet.
  • A caulk bead at the base where local practice calls for it.

What affects the price

The price depends on the problem, the parts and whether the toilet is repaired or replaced. A new flapper is a small job. A new toilet with a flange repair is larger. We quote first and never add parts without asking.

Toilets with the EPA WaterSense label use no more than 1.28 gallons per flush. Vitreous china fixtures are made to ASME A112.19.2, and installation follows the local plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
