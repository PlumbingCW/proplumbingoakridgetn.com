Woodland | Oak Ridge, Anderson County

Plumber in Woodland, Oak Ridge, TN

Woodland is a planned postwar neighborhood south of the Oak Ridge Turnpike, built to a 1948 master plan and opened to residents in 1950. We take plumbing calls here for drains, water heaters, fixtures and leaks.

Where Woodland sits in Oak Ridge

Woodland lies south of the Oak Ridge Turnpike in central Oak Ridge. A National Park Service historic district form places it near Rutgers, Lafayette, Benedict and Wilberforce avenues, with South Illinois Avenue running between it and its neighbor Scarboro. Rutgers Avenue links the neighborhood to the Turnpike.

The form says major city streets bound Woodland, which is one reason it has seen limited modern development. We describe the area by those anchor streets because the exact edges are not something we can promise.

A neighborhood built from one master plan

Woodland was designed by the architects Skidmore, Owings and Merrill for the Atomic Energy Commission and published in a 1948 master plan. It was built in 1949 and opened to occupants in 1950. Residents could begin buying their houses in 1956, and the Park Service form lists the district's period of significance as 1942 to 1959.

Together with Scarboro, the plan produced about 900 houses, ten house types and five apartment types. Woodland has more single-family models, while Scarboro is mostly duplexes.

What this means for a plumbing call

These are modest, mostly one-story houses, and the survey notes that nearly all of them have had their original roofing replaced and some have had a gable roof added. Decades of owners mean decades of repairs. We cannot know what pipe any one Woodland house has, so the plumber looks at the lines in front of them before quoting.

If a drain backs up, the first question is whether the blockage is inside the house or out in the lateral that runs to the city sewer. Where the public side begins is a question for the City of Oak Ridge utility office at (865) 425-3400, and we will work with what the city requires.

Plumbing work we are asked about in Woodland

  • Slow or backed-up sinks, tubs and floor drains, handled through drain cleaning and, for a sewer-side problem, sewer line cleaning.
  • A heater that has stopped making hot water or is leaking, covered by water heater repair and replacement.
  • Water stains, a high bill or a wet crawlspace, where leak detection finds the source before walls are opened.
  • Running toilets and worn fixtures, fixed through toilet repair and faucet repair.

Public sewer work has happened here

In September 2014 the city announced a temporary closure of Marquette Road, between Lafayette Drive and North Purdue Avenue, for sewer rehabilitation work. That tells us public sewer maintenance goes on in Woodland. It does not tell us anything about the pipe inside any one house.

If you see crews on your street and then notice slow drains, mention it when you call. It helps the plumber decide where to look first.

Before you call from Woodland

  • Find the water shutoff and close it if water is flowing.
  • Note which fixtures are slow or backed up, and whether more than one is affected.
  • Move anything stored near the water heater, the sink base or a floor drain.
  • Have the street address ready. Anchor streets such as Rutgers or South Illinois help us confirm the area.

You hear the price before work begins, and if the job changes once a wall or floor is open, the plumber stops and tells you first.
