Tankless Water Heater for an Apartment Building in Oak Ridge, TN

Tankless heaters promise endless hot water and a smaller footprint, which is why apartment owners ask about them. The right answer depends on gas, venting, flow and how your building is laid out.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

An apartment building has a different hot water problem from a house. Demand peaks in the morning and evening, many tenants shower at once and a failure leaves several households cold at the same time. Tankless heaters can help, but they are not a plug-in swap.

Two ways to heat water in a multi-unit building

The first approach is a central system, where one or more large heaters supply the whole building through a loop. The second is a per-unit approach, where each apartment gets its own heater. Tankless units can be used in either setup, but the design differs.

Central versus per-unit
FeatureCentral plantPer unit
Failure impactMany tenants lose hot waterOne tenant affected
Gas or electric billingOwner pays, often passes cost onOften tenant-metered
PipingNeeds a recirculation loopShort runs, no loop
MaintenanceOne place to serviceMany units to service
SpaceA mechanical roomA closet in each unit

Neither is better in every case. A small building with a few units may suit per-unit heaters. A larger building often suits a central plant with several units in cascade, so one failure does not shut off the whole building.

The gas line is usually the first obstacle

A tankless heater burns a lot of gas for a short time. A line that serves a tank heater may be far too small for it. In a multi-unit building, the meter, the main and each branch have to carry the new load. Before anyone orders equipment, the line size, the meter capacity and the gas utility's requirements need to be checked.

Electric tankless units avoid the gas question but can need very large electrical service. In an older building, a panel upgrade may cost more than the heater. We look at both options before suggesting one.

Venting and where the exhaust goes

Each gas tankless unit needs a vent path rated for its exhaust, and high-efficiency units produce condensate that needs a drain. In a building with finished ceilings and shared walls, finding a path for each vent is often harder than finding a place for the unit. This is why a walk-through comes before a quote.

Flow, temperature rise and the winter test

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat by a given number of degrees. Incoming water is colder in winter, so the same unit delivers less hot water in January than in July. A design that works in summer and fails in winter is a common mistake.

Several showers, a dishwasher and a washing machine can run at once in a unit. The heater has to be sized for that peak, not the average. We ask about fixtures and tenant habits as part of any quote.

Questions to answer before you buy

  • How many units and fixtures does the system serve?
  • What is the gas line size and the meter capacity?
  • Where can each unit vent, and where does condensate drain?
  • What is the incoming water temperature in winter?
  • Who maintains the heaters, and how will you flush them?

Hard water and the maintenance question

Scale builds inside a tankless heat exchanger and cuts its efficiency. In a building, that means someone has to flush the units on a schedule. Isolation valves on each unit make this practical. A building with no maintenance plan is a poor candidate for tankless.

Gas tankless units are certified to ANSI Z21.10.3, and multi-unit gas and venting work follows the local plumbing code, based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), plus the gas utility's requirements.

Oak Ridge note

We have not verified a water hardness figure for Oak Ridge, so we do not quote one. If you own a building here, ask the City of Oak Ridge for its latest water quality report at (865) 425-1600 and test the tap in a few units. The result tells you how often to descale.

Rules for gas work and for multi-unit buildings vary. Check with the City of Oak Ridge building office and your gas utility before design work begins. We follow what they require.

When tankless is the wrong answer

A building with a good central tank system that is only a few years old may not benefit from a change. A building with a tiny gas line and no room to upgrade it may not, either. We say so, because a poor tankless install costs more to fix than it ever saved.
