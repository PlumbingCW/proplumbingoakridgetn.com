How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Oak Ridge, TN

The most useful thing to know before a plumbing emergency is where the main shutoff is. Spend ten minutes finding it today, and you will save yourself a bad night later.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

When a pipe bursts, the question is how long the water runs before you stop it. People who know where the main shutoff is stop it in seconds. People who do not may spend ten minutes searching while water runs through the ceiling. A short walk through the house now is the cheapest plumbing insurance there is.

Where the main shutoff usually is

Common locations
House typeWhere to look
BasementOn the wall where the water line enters, often near the meter or the water heater
CrawlspaceNear the front wall where the line comes in, sometimes under a hatch
Slab on gradeIn a utility closet, the garage or beside the water heater
Any houseIn an in-ground box near the street, where the meter sits
Older housePossibly in a cabinet or a closet that was remodeled around it

Follow the water line from where it enters the house. The main shutoff is the first valve after the line enters. It may be a round handle, which is a gate valve, or a lever, which is a ball valve. In the yard, the meter box often holds a second valve that belongs to the utility.

Two valves, two jobs

The valve at the house is yours to use. The valve at the meter box belongs to the water utility, and you should not turn it unless the utility says so. In an emergency, if your valve is stuck, you can contact the utility and ask for help.

How to test the valve

  1. Find the valve and clear the area around it.
  2. Turn the handle clockwise. A ball valve takes a quarter turn. A gate valve takes several full turns.
  3. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house and watch the flow stop.
  4. Turn the valve back on slowly, then check for drips at the stem.
  5. Tag the valve so everyone in the house can find it.

Signs the valve needs replacing

  • It will not turn, or it turns but the water does not stop.
  • It drips around the handle or the stem.
  • The handle is loose, cracked or corroded.
  • It is a gate valve more than a few decades old.
  • You have not turned it in many years.

If the valve is stuck

Do not force a valve that is stuck. A broken stem can leave you with no way to stop the water and a worse leak. If the valve will not move, call a plumber. We can often free it, repack it or replace it with a ball valve. We also tell you whether the pipes around it are sound, because an old valve often sits on old pipe.

In an emergency, the utility can shut off water at the meter, and the dispatcher on our line will tell you who to call.

The other shutoffs worth knowing

  • The valves under each sink and behind each toilet.
  • The valve above the water heater.
  • The shutoff for the washing machine hoses.
  • The shutoff for the outdoor spigots, often inside a basement or crawlspace.
  • The gas shutoff, if you have gas appliances, which is a separate valve.

Fixture shutoffs let you stop a small leak without turning off the whole house. A failed fixture shutoff is a common reason for a bigger job, so replacing a stiff one is worth doing during a repair.

The plumbing code, based on the IPC or the UPC, calls for accessible shutoff valves at fixtures, so older homes may have fewer than a newer one.

Oak Ridge note

The City of Oak Ridge utility office at (865) 425-3400 handles questions about the water meter, with an after-hours line at (865) 425-4399. If you cannot close your own main valve, call them.

Old Oak Ridge homes have gone through many owners and remodels, so the main shutoff may be somewhere unexpected, such as behind a panel or in a closet that was added later. Ask the previous owner or look for a marker on the wall.

Make it a ten-minute habit

Find the valve, test it, tag it and take a photo. Tell everyone in the house. Do it again once a year, since a valve that is never turned will freeze in place. If you have a rental, show your tenants. We serve Oak Ridge, Clinton and nearby towns, and the service area page lists them. If you ever need help with a leak, see burst pipe repair.

Teach everyone in the house

A shutoff only helps if the person home during the leak knows about it. Show every adult and older child where it is and which way it turns. Put a tag on it, write the location on the inside of a cabinet door and keep a flashlight nearby if the valve sits in a dark crawlspace or basement corner.
