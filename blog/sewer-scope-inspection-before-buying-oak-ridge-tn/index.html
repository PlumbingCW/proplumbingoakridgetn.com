Sewer Scope Before Buying a Home in Oak Ridge, TN

A home inspection looks at what can be seen. A sewer scope looks at the pipe that carries everything away. For an older house, it is one of the most useful extra checks a buyer can make.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A buyer sees the kitchen, the roof and the yard. The sewer lateral, the pipe from the house to the street, is buried and out of sight. If it has failed, the cost of repair can be larger than many other problems on the inspection report. A camera run before closing shows what the pipe looks like from the inside.

What the scope is

A plumber feeds a camera on a flexible cable into the line through a cleanout or another access point. The camera sends video to a monitor while a locator at the surface tracks its position and depth. You see roots, cracks, offsets, sags and buildup, and the plumber notes where each one sits.

What a scope can find

Findings and what they mean for a buyer

Why older houses matter most

Oak Ridge grew quickly from the 1940s, and many neighborhoods were built within a few decades. Sewer laterals from those years may be clay, cast iron or fiber pipe, depending on when and where the house was built. We do not judge a line by its age. The camera shows the real condition, and some old lines are in good shape.

If the line is in the final years of its life, a buyer wants to know before closing. The cost of a sewer line repair can be large, and it is a negotiation point that disappears once the sale is done.

What the scope does not tell you

  • It does not show every part of the plumbing, such as supply lines in the walls.
  • It cannot get past a full collapse or a blockage without cleaning first.
  • It shows the lateral from the cleanout, not the public main in the street.
  • It is a snapshot. A line can change over time.

For supply leaks, a different method works. See leak detection.

How to use the video

  1. Ask the seller for access to the cleanout, and tell the listing agent when you book.
  2. Watch the video with the plumber, and ask what each defect means.
  3. Get a written summary and a repair estimate for any defect.
  4. Share both with your agent before the inspection period ends.
  5. Decide whether to ask for a repair, a credit or to walk away.

A seller's agreement to repair a line is a negotiation. If the repair is covered, ask for the work to be done by a licensed contractor, with paperwork.

If there is no cleanout

Many older houses have none. We can often use a toilet flange or another access point, and the plumber may need to pull a toilet. Adding a cleanout is a worthwhile fix, and it makes every future inspection and cleaning easier.

The video is not a code inspection. Repairs that follow it are governed by the local plumbing code, based on the IPC or the UPC, which covers slope and cleanouts, so ask the local office what applies.

Oak Ridge note

The City of Oak Ridge handles the public sewer main, and the homeowner is generally responsible for the lateral from the house to the connection. If the scope shows a problem near the street, ask the City of Oak Ridge utility office at (865) 425-3400 where the responsibility line falls.

In Clinton, the Codes and Zoning office at (865) 457-0424 can answer questions about permits and the public side.

Booking a scope during an inspection window

Inspection periods are short. Call us as soon as you have the address, and we will schedule a camera run that fits your window. We serve Oak Ridge, Clinton and nearby towns, as listed on the service area page.

Timing matters. Most contracts give buyers a short window for inspections, and a scope fits easily into it. Booking the camera run in the same week as the general inspection lets you compare both reports and negotiate once, with the full picture.

Reading the report you receive

A good report names each defect, gives its distance from the cleanout and says whether it is minor, moderate or serious. Minor items, such as a small root at a joint, may need only a cleaning. Serious items, such as a break or a long sag, need a repair quote before you commit. Ask for the video file so your agent and any contractor can see the same thing.
