Reverse Osmosis Faucet Repair for Oak Ridge, TN Homes

The little faucet beside your sink is the part of a reverse osmosis system you touch every day. When it drips or slows to a trickle, the cause is usually small and the repair is usually simple.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A reverse osmosis system filters water through several stages, then stores it in a small tank under the sink. A dedicated faucet delivers that water. Because the system is pressurized by the tank, a faulty faucet can leak without you noticing at first. A slow drip inside the cabinet can ruin the base.

What the parts do

Under the sink you will usually find a feed valve, a set of filter housings, a membrane housing, a storage tank and a series of thin plastic tubes that connect them. The tubes end at the faucet through quick-connect fittings. Knowing which tube does what is half of any repair.

Symptoms and likely causes
SymptomLikely causeFix

Water at the base of the faucetLoose or cracked connectionReseat or replace the fitting
Water in the cabinetLoose tube fitting or housing O-ringReseat the tube, replace the O-ring
Gurgling or constant drainingAir gap or drain saddle problemCheck the drain connection

Start with the filters

A trickle usually means the filters are due. Sediment and carbon filters clog over time, and the membrane has a longer life but also wears out. Check the dates on your housings or the manufacturer's schedule. A set of fresh filters and sanitized housings often restores the flow.

If the flow is still weak after new filters, the storage tank may have lost its air charge. A gauge on the tank valve tells you the pressure when the tank is empty. A plumber can recharge or replace it.

Faucet leaks and loose fittings

The faucet is mounted through a hole in the sink or countertop and connected below with a push-in fitting. A drip at the spout often means the faucet itself is worn. A leak under the sink often means a tube has slipped out of its fitting or was cut at a bad angle.

  1. Close the feed valve and the valve on top of the tank.
  2. Open the RO faucet to relieve pressure and drain the line.
  3. Press the collar on the quick connect fitting and pull the tube out.
  4. Trim a clean, square end on the tube and push it firmly back in.
  5. Reopen the valves slowly and check for drips.

Drain saddles and air gaps

RO systems send reject water to the drain through a saddle clamped on the drain pipe. Many sinks also have an air gap on the faucet itself. If the air gap is clogged, water may spill out of the gap or the unit can gurgle. A clogged saddle can cause a slow kitchen drain.

If your sink also backs up, the clog may be in the drain line and not the filter. See drain cleaning.

A quick maintenance routine

  • Replace sediment and carbon filters on the manufacturer's schedule.
  • Sanitize the system when you change the membrane.
  • Check under the sink each month for damp tubing.
  • Keep the tank valve and feed valve free of obstructions.
  • Mark the filter dates on the cabinet.

Know your water before you buy a system

A reverse osmosis system is a response to a specific concern, such as taste or a measured contaminant. We have not verified a hardness figure for Oak Ridge, and we do not claim the city water needs one. If you are thinking about a system, ask the City of Oak Ridge for its annual water quality report and test your tap first.

Under-sink systems that reduce contaminants should be certified to the NSF/ANSI 58 standard, and the certification sheet tells you what each model is tested to reduce. Check it against what your water report says you need.

Oak Ridge note

The City of Oak Ridge delivers drinking water to its customers and publishes an annual water quality report. The 2020 report stated the water met state and federal drinking water requirements. You can request a paper copy by calling (865) 425-1600.

If your home is on a private well or is outside the city service area, the report does not apply, and a lab test is the way to find out what is in your water.

When to call

Call a plumber if you see water in the cabinet that you cannot trace, if the faucet needs replacing and the countertop is stone or granite or if the system needs a new drain connection.
