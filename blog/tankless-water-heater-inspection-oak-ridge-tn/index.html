Tankless Water Heater Inspection: An Oak Ridge, TN Homeowner Guide

A tankless heater runs hot and works hard, and it tells you when something is wrong if you know how to read it. Here is what an inspection covers and what you can check yourself.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A tankless water heater has no tank to leak, which can make owners forget it needs care. Inside, a heat exchanger carries water past a burner or an electric element, and a controller manages the flow and the temperature. Scale, dust and wear change how well it all works.

What an inspection covers

  • The error code history, which shows what the unit has been reporting.
  • The inlet water filter, which catches debris before it enters the unit.
  • The heat exchanger for scale and the flow rate through it.
  • The vent, the intake and the condensate drain on high-efficiency models.
  • The gas pressure and the burner flame on gas units.
  • The water and gas connections for leaks, and the isolation valves.

A visit also includes a flush, if the unit has isolation valves. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger to dissolve scale, then rinses it out.

How often to flush

The manufacturer's manual sets the schedule, and hard water shortens it. We have not verified a hardness figure for Oak Ridge, so we do not recommend a number. A simple way to decide is to test your tap, ask the City of Oak Ridge for its water quality report and flush more often if scale shows up on fixtures.

What you can check yourself

  1. Look at the display for an error code and write it down.
  2. Turn off the unit and the water, and clean the inlet filter if the model has one.
  3. Check that the vent is clear of nests, snow or debris.
  4. Look for water drops at the connections and the condensate line.
  5. Run a hot tap and note how long it takes to get hot.

Never open the gas side or the combustion chamber unless you know the unit. Many problems need a plumber who works on gas appliances.

Condensate and venting problems

High-efficiency tankless heaters cool their exhaust enough that water condenses in the vent. That condensate drains through a small line, often to a floor drain or a neutralizer. If the line clogs, the unit may shut off with an error. The vent also needs to slope and stay sealed, because exhaust must not enter the house.

Why we pay attention to the vent

A tankless heater produces combustion gases that must leave the building safely. A loose joint, a blocked vent or a backdraft puts exhaust where people breathe it. If you smell gas or exhaust, leave the building, call 911 or your gas utility and then call us for the repair.

When an older tankless is not worth saving

A tankless unit can last for a long time with care. If the heat exchanger has cracked, the control board has failed or scale has damaged it beyond a flush, replacement may be the better answer. We tell you the repair and replacement numbers side by side. See our tankless water heater page for the service options.

Gas tankless heaters are certified to ANSI Z21.10.3, and the manufacturer's maintenance guidelines tell you how often to flush and what to check.

Records worth keeping

Write down the model, the serial number and the date of each flush or repair, and tape the note near the unit. When an error code returns, the history tells a plumber whether scale, the vent or a failing part is the likely cause. Keep the manual too, because the maintenance schedule and the code list are both in it.
