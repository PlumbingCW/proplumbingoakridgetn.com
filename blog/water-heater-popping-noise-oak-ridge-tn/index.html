Water Heater Popping or Rumbling Noise: What It Means in Oak Ridge, TN

A water heater that pops, rumbles or knocks is telling you something about what is inside the tank. Most of the time it is sediment, and most of the time it can be fixed before it becomes a bigger problem.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A tank water heater should be quiet apart from a soft hum or the click of a burner. When it starts to pop like popcorn, rumble like distant thunder or bang when the water is used, something has changed inside. The sounds point to different causes, and some of them are warnings.

What the sound is telling you

Noises and what they usually mean
SoundLikely causeWhat to do
Popping or cracklingSediment trapping water that boils through itFlush the tank
RumblingHeavier sediment or scale layerFlush, check the anode and the elements
Ticking or tappingPipes expanding in heatUsually harmless, check pipe supports
Banging when a tap closesWater hammer in the supply linesAdd arrestors, check pressure
Hissing or sizzlingWater dripping on a hot element or burnerLook for a leak, shut off power or gas
ScreechingValve or fitting partly closedCheck the valves and the flow

Why sediment makes noise

Minerals in water settle to the bottom of the tank over time. On a gas heater, the burner heats the tank floor, and the sediment layer traps water against the hot steel. The trapped water boils and bursts through the layer, which makes the popping you hear. On an electric heater, a lower element can get coated in scale and burn out.

Sediment also insulates the water from the heat, so the heater works harder to do the same job. Over years, that wear shortens the life of the tank and raises your energy use.

The flush

A flush clears much of the sediment. The plumber turns off power or gas, closes the cold supply, connects a hose to the drain valve and drains the tank. Flushing with the supply open stirs the sediment, and the water carries it out. It can take several rounds to run clear.

  1. Turn off the power at the breaker, or set the gas control to pilot or off.
  2. Close the cold water supply to the heater.
  3. Connect a hose to the drain valve and run it to a floor drain or outside.
  4. Open a hot tap in the house so the tank can drain.
  5. Open the drain valve and drain until the water runs clear, then close it and refill.

If the drain valve is plastic and old, it can break or clog. A plumber carries a replacement. A tank that has not been flushed for many years can have so much sediment that the flush clogs the valve, which is another reason to call.

When noise means more than sediment

A tank that rumbles loudly, leaks at the base or has rust-colored water coming from the hot taps may be failing. A flush will not fix a tank with a corroded shell. Our water heater replacement page covers what comes next.

Hard water and heater life

The more minerals in the water, the faster the sediment builds. We have not verified a hardness figure for Oak Ridge, and we do not quote one. If you hear popping soon after a flush, or scale shows up on your fixtures, test your tap, and ask the City of Oak Ridge for its water quality report.

Maintenance that prevents the noise

  • Flush the tank on the schedule in the manual, often once a year.
  • Check the anode rod every few years, and replace it when it is mostly gone.
  • Test the relief valve once a year.
  • Keep the area around the heater clear.
  • Set the thermostat at 120 degrees Fahrenheit unless you have a reason to go higher.

Gas storage water heaters are certified to ANSI Z21.10.1, and the manufacturer's maintenance guidelines in the manual set the flush schedule, so check them first.

Oak Ridge note

The City of Oak Ridge publishes an annual water quality report, and you can request a paper copy by calling (865) 425-1600. It helps you understand what your water carries.

Permit rules for water heater work vary. Ask the City of Oak Ridge building office whether a flush, a part swap or a replacement needs a permit.

Getting it looked at

A visit includes a flush, a check of the anode, the elements or the burner and the relief valve. We tell you what the tank looks like and whether another few years is realistic. We serve Oak Ridge, Clinton and nearby towns, as shown on the service area page.

What the age of the tank tells you

The serial number on the label usually encodes the build date, and the manufacturer's site or manual explains how to read it. A tank that is young and noisy usually needs only a flush. A tank near the end of the range its manufacturer gives, and noisy, is a good time to price a replacement while the heater still works, so a failure does not choose the day for you.
