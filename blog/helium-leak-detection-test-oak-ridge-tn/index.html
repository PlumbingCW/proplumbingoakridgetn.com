Leak detection guide

Helium Leak Detection Test for Oak Ridge, TN Homes

Helium and similar tracer gases can find tiny leaks that water alone leaves hidden. They also cost more than a simple meter test. Here is when each makes sense.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Most hidden leaks start the same way. A water bill climbs, a spot on the floor stays warm or a stain keeps coming back after you paint. Finding the exact spot is the challenge, and the method depends on what kind of line is leaking.

What tracer gas is and how it works

Tracer gas testing fills a pipe with a gas that is lighter than air and harmless to breathe in these amounts. Helium is one choice. A common alternative is a hydrogen and nitrogen blend. The gas escapes through any crack in the line and rises through soil, concrete and flooring. A sensitive detector at the surface picks it up, and the strongest reading marks the spot.

The method works well on drain lines, sewer lines and pipes that can be emptied of water. It can find leaks too small to hear and too slow to see.

We often combine methods. A meter test says there is a leak. A pressure test shows which line. Listening gear and thermal imaging often find the spot. Tracer gas is the tool for the hard cases where those methods are not enough.

Where tracer gas shines

  • A drain or sewer line that leaks under a slab.
  • A supply line that can be isolated, drained and filled with gas.
  • A leak in a wall cavity where listening gear cannot tell which pipe it is.
  • Radiant heating lines under a floor.
  • Confirming a repair held before closing a wall.

What you can do before booking

Cheap tests first

  • Do the meter test: turn off all water and watch the meter for 30 minutes.
  • Check toilets with food coloring in the tank.
  • Look under every sink and around the water heater for dampness.
  • Check outdoor spigots and irrigation lines.
  • Compare your last six water bills for a jump.

If the meter moves with everything off, a leak exists. If it does not, your high bill may come from a running toilet or a seasonal use you forgot. The simple tests cost nothing and often end the search.

What to expect on the day

  1. We confirm the leak exists and which line is suspect.
  2. The plumber isolates and drains the suspect line.
  3. The line is filled with the tracer gas under low pressure.
  4. A detector sweeps the floor, wall or yard to find the strongest signal.
  5. We mark the spot, explain the repair options and give the price.

Older homes and slab construction

Many Oak Ridge homes are built on slabs, and the older ones have supply and drain lines under the concrete. Pipe wear, shifting and soil movement can open a crack decades after construction. Finding the spot before cutting the slab can save hours of demolition, and tracer gas is one tool that makes that possible.

If the supply line proves to be badly corroded in several places, see our repiping page, because repeated spot repairs cost more than a fresh run.

Tracer gas methods use gases such as helium or a hydrogen and nitrogen blend, and the equipment makers publish safe-use guidelines that we follow. The repair that follows is governed by the local plumbing code, based on the IPC or the UPC.

Oak Ridge note

The City of Oak Ridge handles the water meter and the service connection. If a meter test shows water moving and the leak seems to be between the meter and the street, contact the city at (865) 425-1600 to ask who is responsible for that section.

Insurance often covers sudden damage and sometimes the cost of finding a leak. Policies vary, so call your agent before the visit and keep our written findings.

The price question

Tracer gas testing costs more than a meter test because of the equipment and the time. We do not publish a flat price, since it depends on the size of the system and the access. We tell you the price before we begin, and we tell you if a simpler test is likely to do the job.

How we choose a method

We start with the cheapest test that can answer the question. If a meter test shows no movement, we stop there and save you the cost of equipment. If it moves, a pressure test names the line, and listening gear or a thermal camera often finds the spot. We bring out tracer gas when those tools disagree or the pipe is a drain that cannot be pressurized.
