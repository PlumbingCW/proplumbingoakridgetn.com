Frozen Pipes in Oak Ridge, TN: What to Do Before They Burst

A faucet that runs slow or not at all on a cold morning may mean a frozen pipe. The next hour decides whether you have a thaw or a flood. Here is how to handle it.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

Water expands as it freezes, and a pipe cannot stretch to hold the ice. Pressure builds between the ice plug and the closed faucet until the pipe gives way. The split often appears where the ice has formed, but water does not flow out until the ice melts. That is why a thaw is the moment of greatest risk.

If you see a crack or bulge, shut off the main water first, then call us. See burst pipe repair.

How to thaw a frozen pipe safely

  1. Open the faucet served by the pipe, so the water has somewhere to go as ice melts.
  2. Find the frozen section. It is usually on an outside wall, in a crawlspace or near a vent.
  3. Warm the pipe with a hair dryer on low, working from the faucet end toward the frozen part.
  4. You can also wrap the pipe in towels soaked in warm water, or use a heat lamp at a safe distance.
  5. Keep the faucet open until water flows normally, and check for leaks along the pipe.

Never do these

  • Do not use an open flame such as a torch or lighter. It can ignite framing or damage the pipe.
  • Do not leave a heat source unattended.
  • Do not thaw a pipe you can see is split.
  • Do not use a space heater near wet floors.
  • Do not ignore a pipe that stays frozen after an hour of careful warming.

When the thaw does not work

If the frozen section is inside a wall or under a floor, you cannot reach it. A plumber can thaw a pipe in place, find the freeze and, if it has split, repair it. We also check the other pipes in the same run, since those that froze once are likely to freeze again.

Why pipes freeze in a mild climate

East Tennessee has cold snaps that arrive faster than homes are prepared for. Homes with crawlspaces, supply lines in outside walls, uninsulated garages and kitchens on a north wall are the usual victims. A pipe that has frozen before is a sign of a weak spot, such as a missing insulation sleeve or a gap in the wall.

Oak Ridge sits among ridges and valleys, and cold air pools in low spots. Two houses on the same street can have different risks. Check the exposed pipes in your own crawlspace or garage and not just the weather forecast.

Prevent the next freeze

  • Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces and attics with foam sleeves.
  • Seal drafts around pipes where they enter outside walls.
  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off outdoor spigots from inside, if they have a shutoff.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze.
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest nights, so water keeps moving.
  • Know where your main shutoff is, and test it before winter.

If you will be away during a freeze, ask someone to check the house, and consider leaving the heat on at a safe setting.

Use insulation sleeves and heat tape according to the manufacturer's guidelines. Electric heat tape needs ground fault protection under the National Electrical Code (NEC), so check the label before you plug it in.
