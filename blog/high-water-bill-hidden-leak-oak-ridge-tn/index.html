High Water Bill in Oak Ridge, TN: How to Find a Hidden Leak

A high water bill is the first sign most people get of a hidden leak. Before you call anyone, a half hour with the meter can tell you whether water is escaping and where to look.

By the Oak Ridge Pro Plumbing team · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 16, 2026

A water bill is a monthly report on how much water passed through your meter. If it doubles and your habits did not change, water is going somewhere. It might be a running toilet, a dripping outdoor line, an irrigation timer or a pipe leaking inside a wall or under a slab.

The meter test

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and ice maker. Make sure no one is using water.
  2. Find the meter, usually in a box near the street or at the front of the property.
  3. Lift the lid. Note the position of the small leak indicator, a triangle or a small wheel, or write down the reading.
  4. Wait 30 minutes, and use no water in that time.
  5. Look again. Movement of the indicator or a changed reading means water is leaving the system.

If nothing moves, the leak may be intermittent, such as a toilet that runs now and then or an irrigation system that runs on a timer. If it moves, the next step is to find which line.

Narrowing down the zone

If the meter moves and the toilets and faucets pass, try closing the main valve at the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is in the yard line between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside, and you can close the branch valves one by one to find the zone.

A leak that shows up only on the hot side points to the water heater or the hot lines. A warm spot on the floor can mean a hot water line under a slab.

What a high bill can and cannot tell you

A bill is not proof of a leak. Visitors, a new fixture, a filled pool or watering during a dry spell can raise it. Compare your usage with the same month last year, and ask the City of Oak Ridge whether the meter was read correctly. Read the bill closely before you spend money.

What helps when you call us

  • The meter test result and the time it took.
  • Whether the toilets pass the dye test.
  • Whether any spot on the floor is warm, wet or soft.
  • Whether you hear running water when everything is off.
  • Your last few bills, if you have them.

What a plumber will do

We confirm the leak with a pressure test, then narrow it with listening gear and, where useful, thermal imaging or tracer gas. The goal is to find the leak before opening a wall. When a pipe is badly corroded in several places, we tell you plainly, and our repiping page covers the longer answer.

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, a toilet dye test and a meter check are the standard first steps for finding a household leak, and both cost nothing.

Oak Ridge note
