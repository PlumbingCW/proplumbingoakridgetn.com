Prepare before the plumber arrives

01

Find the main shutoff

It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

02

Clear a path

Move boxes and furniture away from the water heater, the sink or the cleanout so the plumber can reach it.

03

Take photos

Photograph the damage and any standing water before you clean up. Your insurer will ask.

04

Note what you saw

Write down when it started, which fixtures are affected and what you heard or smelled. It speeds up the diagnosis.

A river town with housing from several eras

Clinton is the county seat of Anderson County and sits on the Clinch River, with Market Street running through the historic center and US 25W and State Route 61 carrying traffic through town. A landmark on one of those routes helps the dispatcher find you quickly.

The Green McAdoo Cultural Center, which preserves the story of the school integration that happened here in the 1950s, is a reminder that the town has been settled for generations. In practical terms, older homes near the center may still have galvanized or cast iron lines, while newer subdivisions have plastic and copper. We check the pipe in front of us and do not assume from the street.

Why it matters for your repair

Older lines corrode from the inside and clog more often at joints. Newer homes have different weak points, such as fittings and appliance hoses. The right fix depends on which one you have, so we look before we quote, and we tell you if a camera look would help.

If the line from the house to the street is the problem, who is responsible depends on where it fails. Ask the City of Clinton Codes and Zoning office at (865) 457-0424 about permits and the public side, and we will work with what they require. Repairs follow the plumbing code the local office has adopted, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
