Main sewer line cleaning

Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Oak Ridge, TN

When the line that carries everything out of the house stops, every drain feels it. We clear the main with heavy cable equipment, check the result and tell you whether cleaning is enough.

The main sewer line is the largest drain on the property. It collects every fixture and carries the flow to the connection at the street or the septic system. When it clogs, the lowest drain in the house fills first, often a basement floor drain or a ground-level shower.

Main line backup: what to do first

Unlike a sink clog, a main line problem shows up in more than one place. Toilets bubble when the washer drains. The bathtub gurgles when you flush. In bad cases, a cleanout cap in the yard has water standing in it. If you see that, do not remove the cap. Call us.

What to do right now

  • Stop running water, including dishwashers and washing machines.
  • Do not flush toilets, since each flush adds to the overflow.
  • Keep children and pets away from any wet floor.
  • Move belongings off the floor near backed-up drains.
  • Call us and describe which fixtures are affected.

What clogs a main line

Tree roots are the most common cause. They enter through joints, thicken and catch paper and wipes. Grease and soap coat the pipe wall until the opening narrows. Flushed wipes, even those labeled flushable, tangle on roots and pile up. In some lines, a sag or break holds debris that the water cannot move.

Cleaning versus repair

Cleaning restores the flow but it does not fix a cracked pipe or a bad joint. If roots come back every few months, or the camera finds a break, repeated cleaning is money spent without a cure. We tell you when it is time to move to sewer line repair.

Some homes benefit from a preventive schedule, such as an annual cleaning when roots are known to be a factor. We do not recommend one for every house, and a camera look tells you whether it makes sense.

Septic and municipal systems

Many homes in and around Oak Ridge connect to the city sewer, while some outlying properties use septic. The cleaning approach differs. On a septic property, a backup can point to a full tank or a failed drain field, and cleaning the line will not cure it. We ask which system you have when you call.

The cleaning visit

  1. We locate the cleanout, or another access point if none exists.
  2. The cable runs the line and clears the blockage.
  3. We run water to confirm that it flows freely.
  4. A camera run shows the result when it is useful.
  5. You get a clear report and a plan to keep the line open.

What affects the price

Distance to the blockage, access, tools needed and the hour of the call affect the price. Having a cleanout makes the work faster. We quote before starting and we pause to talk with you if the camera shows damage.

Plumbing codes based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) generally call for cleanouts on building drains. If your line has none, adding one is worth doing, because it makes every future cleaning and camera look easier.
